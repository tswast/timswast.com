Crafting a Digital Pencil: A Gemini & Blender Collaboration

Tim Swena on March 6, 2025

I created a 3D pencil by blending AI and traditional 3D modeling: I used Gemini to generate shapes and Blender to add the finishing touches, resulting in a low-poly-ready model.

Recently, I decided to tackle a fun little project: creating a 3D model of a pencil for a web page. It turned out to be a really interesting exercise in combining AI assistance with traditional 3D modeling.

My initial thought was to see if I could get Gemini to handle the entire process. I started by asking for a model of a pencil. It kinda worked, but wasn't three dimensional at all. As I asked it to add components, like an eraser and a graphite tip, it ended up making this monstrosity:

Undeterred, I decided to take a more methodical approach. I broke the pencil down into its individual shapes and asked Gemini to generate the OBJ and MTL one piece at a time. This worked surprisingly well! By focusing on each component separately, I was able to get accurate and usable models for the hexagonal prism, eraser, band, and cone and stitch them together.

However, there was one part that Gemini struggled with: the graphite tip. No matter how I phrased my requests, I couldn't get it to produce a satisfactory result. That's where Blender came in.

With the smallest bit of manual modeling in Blender, I was able to quickly add slice the cone for the graphite tip and integrate it with the rest of the pencil. It was a good reminder that while AI can be incredibly helpful, sometimes you just need to roll up your sleeves and do things the old-fashioned way.

The entire process was a great learning experience. It showed me the power of using AI as a tool for rapid prototyping and shape generation. It also highlighted the importance of knowing when to switch to traditional methods when AI falls short.

The final result? A simple but effective 3D pencil model and corresponding material file that I'm pretty happy with. I could definitely see this having a place in a low poly video game. It's a testament to the power of combining AI with human creativity. And, of course, a little bit of patience.

While it likely would have been faster just to create the model in Blender, Gemini help reduce the learning curve and didn't take as much mental energy to get started. I'm excited to continue exploring the possibilities of AI-assisted 3D modeling in future projects. Who knows what I'll create next?
